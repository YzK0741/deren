## Continuation: the independent review of the whole batch, and its two misreadings

A second independent review then covered origin/master..HEAD (a5ff51b0..e3bce691) plus the two uncommitted working-tree changes, reading the sources, the built SPIR-V and the offsets by hand. Its verdict was 1 blocker, 2 important, 5 minor; both uncommitted changes were found correct. Three of the findings were real and are fixed; two were misreadings, and both were settled with a test rather than an argument.

BLOCKER - THE DECLARED PUSH BLOCK WAS 12 BYTES SHORTER THAN THE TRANSMITTED ONE. This pass's endpoint is runtime::push_stage_block, which calls push_with_lanes(..., 3u): the frame slot, the image index and the post chain's own source slot are copied in AFTER whatever a pass hands over (source/engine/runtime/runtime.cpp). Every converted stage declares those lanes - shadow.slang carries the note about the cascade having to sit after all three - and gaussian_raster.slang was the only one that did not, so the block the shader DECLARED ended at 108 while the pass TRANSMITTED 124 bytes. Legal while nothing there reads a lane, and a read past the declared block the moment the heap accessors are used. Fixed by declaring the lanes at 112/116/120: spirv-dis on the rebuilt gaussian_raster.vert.spv shows members at 0/16/32/48/64/80/88/96/100/104/108/112/116/120, so the declared block now spans what the endpoint sends while the 112-byte host struct and every offset it asserts are unchanged.

RED FIRST, because nothing in the build compared declared against transmitted size: test_shader_sources grew a lane contract - a pass that pushes a block loads only stages that declare the index lanes - derived from the tree rather than tabulated (a pass source names the .spv it loads, the canonical list names the .slang that produces it, and that source must declare frame_slot and image_index). It failed on exactly this stage before the shader changed and passes after, and its own coverage is asserted, because the passes whose shaders the APP registers are bound too far away for the scan.

IMPORTANT, REFUTED - THE NEAR/FAR PLANE TESTS WERE REPORTED TO DIVERGE BY A WORLD UNIT. The reading was that both sides drop the planes' w term and that the radius needs |n|, leaving the reference keeping splats up to a world unit beyond the far plane while the shader culled them. Both sides in fact use the full homogeneous dot (plane.xyz * world.xyz + plane.w) and the UNNORMALIZED plane normal in the quadratic form, which is what carries |n| into the radius and makes the inequality a world-space sphere test; the one-world-unit slack came from evaluating a normalized normal instead. Rather than argue arithmetic, test_gaussian_raster now transcribes the shader's own rejection and pins six cases around both planes - control, support crossing the far plane from either side, entirely beyond it, the centre on the near plane, support reaching it - asserting the expected verdicts AND that the reference agrees with the transcribed rule in every one (153 checks, 0 failed). The semantics are as documented: the far plane culls only a support entirely beyond it, the near plane culls on contact.

MINOR, REFUTED - "bytes stays 0 while buffers exist when visible_count == 0". The zero-count path returns before any buffer is created (they are created later in the same function), and bytes == 0 there is the documented zero-count/no-GPU-allocation behaviour rather than an accounting gap.

The remaining minors are addressed: the byte arithmetic that sizes the instance and reference uploads is checked before it multiplies, the way pack_asset already does it; projection_visibility now says that `outside` covers a centre behind the camera; docs/gaussian_splatting.md separates the 112-byte host struct from the 124-byte transmitted block and no longer claims a SPIR-V step validates the push offsets (none does - the host offsets are asserted by the test, the shader side by the lane contract, and the emitted offsets were confirmed by hand with spirv-dis); and the new ci.yml tidy entries were re-indented to match their neighbours.
